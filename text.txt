THE IMPACT OF GUIDANCE AND COUNSELLING IN LEARNERS’ ACADEMIC SUCCESS: A CASE OF MPONDASI COMMUNITY DAY SECONDARY SCHOOL.
Introduction
Guidance and counselling services play a critical role in improving learners’ academic achievement, discipline, career awareness, emotional stability, and social behaviour in secondary schools. In Malawi, especially in Community Day Secondary Schools (CDSSs), learners face numerous challenges such as poverty, peer pressure, teenage pregnancies, drug abuse, lack of parental support, examination anxiety, and poor learning environments. These challenges negatively affect learners’ academic success.
At Mpondasi Community Day Secondary School, guidance and counselling can be viewed as an important educational support system that helps learners overcome academic, social, psychological, and career-related challenges. The study therefore evaluates how effective guidance and counselling services contribute to learners’ academic success in a Malawian CDSS context.
Research conducted in different African countries confirms that effective guidance and counselling improves learners’ academic performance, discipline, motivation, and school retention. 
Background of the Study
Guidance and counselling emerged as an educational support service aimed at helping learners understand themselves, make proper decisions, and solve personal and academic problems. In Malawi, guidance and counselling services are increasingly recognized as essential in secondary schools due to rising cases of school dropout, indiscipline, substance abuse, depression, examination stress, and poor academic performance.
Community Day Secondary Schools (CDSSs) in Malawi often experience:
inadequate teaching and learning resources,
overcrowded classrooms,
shortage of trained teachers,
low student motivation,
poverty-related absenteeism,
weak parental involvement.
These factors contribute to poor academic outcomes among learners. Studies in Malawi have shown that proper counselling programmes help learners cope with educational pressures and improve school adjustment. �

Statement of the Problem
Despite the existence of guidance and counselling programmes in many Malawian secondary schools, learners in most CDSSs continue to register poor academic performance in national examinations such as the Junior Certificate of Education (JCE) and Malawi School Certificate of Education (MSCE).
At Mpondasi Community Day Secondary School, learners may still experience:
absenteeism,
poor study habits,
indiscipline,
examination anxiety,
lack of academic motivation,
peer pressure,
substance abuse.
This raises concern regarding the effectiveness and implementation of guidance and counselling services in improving academic success.
The study therefore seeks to evaluate the extent to which guidance and counselling contributes to learners’ academic achievement at Mpondasi CDSS.

Example in a Malawian CDSS Context
At Mpondasi CDSS, learners who receive counselling on:
study timetables,
note-taking techniques,
examination preparation,
peer influence,
may become more focused and motivated, leading to better MSCE results.
2. Reduction of Indiscipline
Indiscipline negatively affects learning environments in many CDSSs. Common problems include:
truancy,
fighting,
bullying,
drug abuse,
disrespect toward teachers.
Counselling helps learners understand consequences of negative behaviour and promotes positive conduct.
For example:
learners involved in bullying may receive behavioural counselling,
learners abusing alcohol or marijuana may undergo rehabilitation-oriented counselling,
learners with anger issues may receive emotional support.
This creates a peaceful learning environment that supports academic success.
3. Enhancement of Self-Esteem and Motivation
Many learners in CDSSs come from poor families and may feel inferior compared to students from conventional secondary schools.
Guidance and counselling help learners:
believe in their abilities,
remain hopeful,
set life goals,
stay motivated.
Research indicates that learner self-management and self-efficacy positively influence academic achievement. �

4. Career Guidance and Academic Direction
Career counselling helps learners understand:
subject combinations,
career pathways,
college requirements,
employment opportunities.
In Malawi, many learners in CDSSs lack exposure to career information. Guidance programmes can:
invite professionals,
organize career talks,
guide subject selection.
This motivates learners to work harder academically.
Example
A learner aspiring to become a nurse may become more serious in Biology and English after receiving career guidance.
5. Psychological and Emotional Support
Many secondary school learners experience:
depression,
family problems,
relationship stress,
examination anxiety.
Without counselling support, these issues may lead to:
poor concentration,
absenteeism,
school dropout.
Effective counselling helps learners cope emotionally and maintain academic focus.
Challenges Affecting Guidance and Counselling in CDSSs
Despite its importance, guidance and counselling in many Malawian CDSSs face several challenges:
1. Lack of Trained Counsellors
Most schools assign counselling duties to ordinary teachers without professional training.
2. Inadequate Resources
Schools often lack:
counselling offices,
privacy,
counselling materials,
career resource books.
3. Negative Attitudes
Some learners fear being stigmatized when seeking counselling.
4. Heavy Teacher Workload
Teacher counsellors are often overloaded with teaching responsibilities.
5. Poverty and Home Problems

Conclusion
Guidance and counselling significantly contribute to learners’ academic success by improving discipline, motivation, emotional stability, study habits, and career awareness. In Community Day Secondary Schools such as Mpondasi Community Day Secondary School, counselling services are especially important because learners face numerous socio-economic and psychological challenges.
Although challenges such as lack of trained personnel and inadequate resources continue to affect implementation, effective guidance and counselling programmes can greatly improve academic performance and learner well-being in Malawi’s CDSSs.
The study therefore concludes that strengthening guidance and counselling services is essential for improving educational outcomes in Malawian secondary schools.
